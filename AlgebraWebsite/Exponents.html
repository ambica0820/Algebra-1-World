Exponents

What are Exponents?

Exponents: mean repeated multiplication

Base Number: is the BIG number you start with.

We can solve exponents by multiplying the base number by itself based on the exponent value.

Exponent Rules

When solving exponents, there are rules that you must follow. Look at the table below for those rules.

Name of the Rule Rule
Product Rule am × an = am+n
Quotient Rule am ÷ an = am-n
Power Rule (am)n = am×n
Zero Exponent Rule a0 = 1
Negative Exponent Rule a-n = 1 ÷ an
Power of a Product Rule (ab)n = anbn

Some Examples

Example 1: 23 = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8

Example 2: 52 = 5 × 5 = 25

Example 3: 40 = 1

Example 4: 2-3 = 1 / 23 = 1 / 8

Example 5: 62 = 6 × 6 = 36

Examples of Exponents

Example 1: 2 3

Here, 2 is the BASE number and 3 is the EXPONENT number.

To figure out what is 23 is equal to, we multiply 2 by itself 3 times. This is because our exponent is 3. So, if we do 2 × 2 × 2 we get 8 because 2 × 2 = 4 and 4 × 2 = 8.

Example 2: 52

To solve 52, we do 5 times itself 2 times because 2 is the exponent. When we do 5 × 5, we get 25. Therefore, 52 is 25.

WARNING: Exponents does NOT mean the base ×exponent. 52 does NOT mean 5 × 2! That is a common mistake.
