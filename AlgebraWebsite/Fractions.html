What is a fraction?

Fractions: a part of a whole or any quantity divided into equal parts.

What are the parts of a fraction called?

There are 2 parts to a fraction. They are:

  • Numerator : is the top number you see in a fraction

  • Denominator : is the bottom number you see in a fraction

Numerator And Denominator

Example 1: 3/4

In the example above, 3 is the numerator, and 4 is the denominator.

Example 2: 5/9

In the example above, 5 is the numerator, and 9 is the denominator.

Example 3: 2/7

In the example above, 2 is the numerator, and 7 is the denominator.

Proper Fractions

Proper Fraction: A fraction where the numerator is smaller than the denominator.

In a proper fraction, the value of the fraction is always less than 1.

Examples of Proper Fractions

Example 1: 3/5

3 is smaller than 5, so 3/5 is a proper fraction.

Example 2: 2/8

2 is smaller than 8, so 2/8 is a proper fraction.

How to Identify a Proper Fraction

  1. Look at the numerator (top number).
  2. Look at the denominator (bottom number).
  3. If the numerator is smaller than the denominator, it is a proper fraction.

Improper Fractions

Improper Fraction: A fraction where the numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator.

In an improper fraction, the value of the fraction is always greater than or equal to 1.

Examples of Improper Fractions

Example 1: 7/4

7 is greater than 4, so 7/4 is an improper fraction.

Example 2: 5/4

5 is greater than 4, so 5/4 is also an improper fraction.

Mixed Numbers

Mixed Number: A number that contains a whole number and a proper fraction.

A mixed number represents a value greater than 1.

Parts of a Mixed Number

A mixed number has two parts:

  • Whole Number: The number before the fraction.

  • Proper Fraction: The fraction part of the mixed number.

How to Identify a Mixed Number

  1. Look for a whole number.
  2. Look for a proper fraction next to it.
  3. If both are present, it is a mixed number.

Examples of Mixed Numbers

Example 1: 2 1/3

In this example, 2 is the whole number and 1/3 is the fraction.

Example 2: 4 3/5

In this example, 4 is the whole number and 3/5 is the fraction.

Mixed Numbers and Improper Fractions

A mixed number can be written as an improper fraction.

To get a mixed number to become an improper fraction, we multiply the denominator with the whole number. Add that product to the numerator. Keep the denominator as it is.

Example:

2 1/3 = 7/3

Explanation:

  1. Identify the denominator. In this example, the denominator is 3.
  2. Multiply the whole number by the denominator:
    2 × 3 = 6
  3. Add the product to the numerator:
    6 + 1 = 7
  4. Keep the denominator the same:
    The denominator stays 3.
  5. The improper fraction is:
    7/3

Equivalent Fractions

Equivalent Fractions : are fractions that look different but have the exact same value

  • Example 1: 2/7 is the same as 4/14

    Explanation: This is because if we were to multiply both the numerator and denominator by 2 we would get 2 × 2 which is 4 and then 7 × 2 which is 14. So 2/7 is equal to 4/14.

  • Example 2: 3/6 is the same as 1/2

    Explanation: This is because if we were to divide both the numerator and denominator by 3 we would get 6/3 which is 2 and 3/3 which is 1. So 3/6 is equal to 1/2.

Adding Fractions (Like Denominators)

Like Denominators: are terms with the same denominator.

Example 1:

3/5 and 2/5 are both like denominators because both have the same denominators.

Example 2:

2/9 and 1/9 are both like denominators because both have the same denominators.

Example 3:

7/8 and 5/8 are both like denominators because both have the same denominators.

Adding Like Denominator Steps:

  1. Add the numerators.
  2. Keep the denominator the same.

Example 1:

2/9 + 1/9

Step 1: We add the numerators. 2 + 1 = 3.

Step 2:We keep the denominators. In this case, it is 9.

Step 3: Our answer is 3/9, which simplifies to 1/3.

Example 2:

5/6 + 3/6

Step 1: We add the numerators. 5 + 3 = 8.

Step 2:We keep the denominators. In this case, it is 6.

Step 3: Our answer is 8/6, which simplifies to 4/3.

Example 3:

2/5 + 3/5

Step 1: We add the numerators. 2 + 3 = 5.

Step 2:We keep the denominators. In this case, it is 5.

Step 3: Our answer is 5/5, which simplifies to 1.

Adding Fractions (Unlike Denominators)

Adding unlike fractions is a bit different because the fractions have different denominators. We cannot add the fractions until they have the same denominator.

Follow the steps below to add unlike fractions:

  1. Find the LCM (Least Common Multiple) of the denominators. The LCM will become the new common denominator.
  2. Rewrite each fraction as an equivalent fraction with the common denominator.
  3. Add the numerators and keep the denominator the same.
  4. Simplify the fraction if needed.

Examples

Example 1: 6/7 + 2/3

  1. The LCM of 7 and 3 is 21.
  2. Writing it as equivalent fractions, we get 18/21 for the 1st fraction and 14/21 for the 2nd fraction.
  3. We now have same denominator fraction. Now we just add the numerators. 18 + 14 is 32.
  4. Our answer is 32/21.

Example 2: 7/3 + 2/6

  1. The LCM of 3 and 6 is 6.
  2. Writing it as equivalent fractions, we get 14/6 for the 1st fraction and 2/6 for the 2nd fraction.
  3. We now have same denominator fraction. Now we just add the numerators. 14 + 2 is 16.
  4. Our answer is 16/6 or in simplified form its 8/3.

Example 3: 3/4 + 2/5

  1. The LCM of 4 and 5 is 20.
  2. Writing it as equivalent fractions, we get 15/20 for the 1st fraction and 8/20 for the 2nd fraction.
  3. We now have same denominator fraction. Now we just add the numerators. 15 + 8 is 23.
  4. Our answer is 23/20.

Example 4: 5/8 + 3/6

  1. The LCM of 8 and 6 is 24.
  2. Writing it as equivalent fractions, we get 15/24 for the 1st fraction and 12/24 for the 2nd fraction.
  3. We now have same denominator fraction. Now we just add the numerators. 15 + 12 = 27.
  4. Our answer is 27/24. Simplest Form: 9/8

Subtracting Fractions(Like Denominators)

To subtract fractions with like (same) denominators, we do the following steps:

  1. Subtract the numerators.
  2. Keep the denominators the same.

Example 1:

2/9 - 1/9

Step 1: We subtract the numerators. 2 - 1 = 1.

Step 2:We keep the denominators. In this case, it is 9.

Step 3: Our answer is 1/9.

Example 2:

4/9 - 5/9

Step 1: We subtract the numerators. 4 - 5 = - 1.

Step 2:We keep the denominators. In this case, it is 9.

Step 3: Our answer is -1/9.

Example 3:

13/4 - 15/4

Step 1: We subtract the numerators. 13 - 15 = - 2.

Step 2:We keep the denominators. In this case, it is 4.

Step 3: Our answer is - 2/4.

Example 4:

29/16 - 23/16

Step 1: We subtract the numerators. 29 - 23 = 6.

Step 2:We keep the denominators. In this case, it is 16.

Step 3: Our answer is 6/16.

Subtracting Fractions with Different Denominators

When fractions have different denominators, we cannot subtract them right away. First, we need to make the denominators the same.

Steps:

  1. Find the Least Common Denominator (LCD).
  2. Rewrite each fraction with the LCD.
  3. Subtract the numerators.
  4. Simplify the fraction if needed.

Example 1:

3/4 - 1/6

  1. Find the LCD of 4 and 6. The LCD is 12
  2. Rewrite both fractions. 3/4 = 9/12 and 1/6 = 2/12
  3. Step 3: Subtract the numerators. 9/12 - 2/12 = 7/12
  4. Step 4: Check if the answer can be simplified. 7/12 cannot be simplified.

The answer is 7/12.

Example 2:

8/7 - 7/8

  1. Find the LCD of 7 and 8. The LCD is 56.
  2. Rewrite both fractions. 8/7 = 64/56 and 7/8 = 49/56.
  3. Step 3: Subtract the numerators. 64/56 - 49/56 = 15/56/
  4. Step 4: Check if the answer can be simplified. 15/56 cannot be simplified.

The answer is 15/56.

Example 3:

2/5 - 9/10

  1. Find the LCD of 5 and 10. The LCD is 10.
  2. Rewrite both fractions. 2/5 = 4/10 and 9/10 = 9/10.
  3. Step 3: Subtract the numerators. (4/10) - (9/10) which is -5/10.
  4. Step 4: Check if the answer can be simplified. -5/10 can be simplified to -1/2.

The answer is -1/2.

Multiplying Fractions

Formula: (Numerator * Numerator) / (Denominator * Denominator)

Remember to simplify if possible!

Example 1: 4/5 × 2/3

  1. First we multiply the numerators. 4 * 2 is 8.
  2. Next, we multiply the denominators. 5 * 3 is 15.
  3. Our answer is 8/15. We see if the answer can be simplified.
  4. 8/15 can NOT be simplied.

Final answer: 8/15.

Example 2: - 4/10 × 9/12

  1. First we multiply the numerators. -4 * 9 is -36.
  2. Next, we multiply the denominators. 10 * 12 is 120.
  3. Our answer is -36/120. We see if the answer can be simplified.
  4. -36/120 can be simplied to -3/10 when we divide both the numerator and denominator by the common factor of 12.

Final answer: -3/10.

Example 3: 7/8 × 6/9

  1. First we multiply the numerators. 7 * 6 is 42.
  2. Next, we multiply the denominators. 8 * 9 is 72..
  3. Our answer is 42/72. We see if the answer can be simplified.
  4. 42/72 can be simplied to 7/12 if we divide both the numerator and denominator by 6.

Final answer: 7/12.

Dividing Fractions

When dividing fractions, we use the Keep, Change, Flip (KCF) formula!

STEPS:
  1. Keep the first fraction the same.
  2. Change the division sign (÷) to multiplication (×).
  3. Flip the second fraction (find its reciprocal).
  4. Multiply the numerators.
  5. Multiply the denominators.
  6. Simplify the answer if needed.

Example 1: 2/3 ÷ 4/5

  1. Keep 2/3 as is.
  2. Change the ÷ to ×
  3. Flip the 2nd fraction: 4/5 becomes 5/4
  4. Now we have 2/3 × 5/4
  5. Multiply across: 2 * 5 is 10 and 3 * 4 is 12.
  6. We get 10/12 as the answer. Now see if we can simplify.
  7. 10/12 can be simplified by dividing by 2 on both numbers. After dividing both 10 and 12 by 2 we get 5/6.

Final Answer: 5/6

Example 2: - 9/10 ÷ 3/7

  1. Keep -9/10 as is.
  2. Change the ÷ to ×
  3. Flip the 2nd fraction: 3/7 becomes 7/3
  4. Now we have -9/10 × 7/3
  5. Multiply across: -9 * 7 is -63 and 10 * 3 is 30.
  6. We get -63/30 as the answer. Now see if we can simplify.
  7. -63/30 can be simplified by dividing by 3 on both numbers. After dividing both -63 and 30 by 3 we get -21/10.

Final Answer: -21/10

Example 3: 6/7 ÷ 20/3

  1. Keep 6/7 as is.
  2. Change the ÷ to ×
  3. Flip the 2nd fraction: 20/3 becomes 3/20
  4. Now we have 6/7 × 3/20
  5. Multiply across: 6 * 3 is 18 and 7 * 20 is 140.
  6. We get 18/140 as the answer. Now see if we can simplify.
  7. 18/140 can be simplified by dividing by 2 on both numbers. After dividing both 18 and 140 by 2 we get 9/70.

Final Answer: 9/70
